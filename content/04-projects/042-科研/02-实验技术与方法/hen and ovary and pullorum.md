#### Summary

Conducting literature reviews involves a systematic and dialogical approach, treating literature as a dataset for qualitative analysis. The process includes searching, identifying, summarizing, compiling, analyzing, interpreting, and referencing existing research works to identify gaps and propose future research agendas. It is critical to ensure rigor by justifying the scope, relevance, and quality of the selected literature. Literature reviews can be curated innovatively and contribute significantly to advancing knowledge by highlighting areas for future research and synthesizing findings through mixed methods research synthesis [[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3fdb1cee0944e08db1380ca0532390109aee1e32 "The Science of Literature Reviews: Searching, Identifying, Selecting, and Synthesising")], [[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b4456d61607a8439081e9f6b8e24080d12a45df "Achieving Rigor in Literature Reviews: Insights from Qualitative Data Analysis and Tool-Support")], [[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0e3eeaa3ad275ef05794661e41eb56301db1a43 "Literature Reviews: Generative and Transformative Textual Conversations")].Debates enhance critical thinking, public speaking, and active listening by engaging students in contemporary, real-world topics [[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c40aed487b78b5fd00c94dea1b5caed76db1f984 "Public debate format for the development of soft skill competency in computer science curricula")]. They are effective for teaching controversial subjects, encouraging active learning over traditional lectures [[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ef0e807f2ae8a3e59db408a31ab493c1aa88cf3 "Debate Preparation/Participation: An Active, Effective Learning Tool")]. Key skills include structuring speeches, argumentation, rebuttal, evidence use, and preparation [[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae975c9536a03b9865c2476f280e44747f6e7474 "Discovering the world through debate : a practical guide to educational debate for debaters, coaches, and judges")]. Structured classroom debates (SCDs) promote participation and critical analysis, accommodating different abilities and class sizes through effective team and role management, with various grading methods [[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e318f29f44b0e05f16bf4ee04c95f81ac2449a7d "Let's Debate: Active Learning Encourages Student Participation and Critical Thinking")]. Debates also improve pre-service teachers' learning and critical thinking in higher education, showing significant benefits in teacher training contexts [[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ce7d7a7fb74bc03c02ba9c1b0c01e3071e0be341 "Using Debate As A Pedagogical Tool In Enhancing Pre-Service Teachers Learning And Critical Thinking")]. Implementing debates in the classroom supports the development of argumentative competence, particularly in teaching English as a Foreign Language, by exploring social issues [[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e5dc6a4e7799147055033864f95fe5f5cbbd5cf1 "Using Debates in the Classroom: A Pedagogical Strategy for the Development of the Argumentative Competence in the Teaching of English as a Foreign Language")].Pullorum disease, caused by *Salmonella serovar Pullorum*, can lead to colonization of the reproductive tract in chickens, affecting both the ovary and oviduct. This colonization can result in infected progeny through transovarian transmission, with up to 6% of laid eggs being infected [[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5ae523c08c6c8b08063a595090377b3712ad3db6 "Salmonella enterica Serovar Pullorum Persists in Splenic Macrophages and in the Reproductive Tract during Persistent, Disease-Free Carriage in Chickens")]. Studies have shown that Leghorn hens infected with *Salmonella Enteritidis* demonstrate the bacteria's ability to colonize various sections of the avian reproductive tract, including the ovary and different parts of the oviduct [[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/79e0dc6894751a7a52411069dd0068f656397460 "Colonization of Avian Reproductive-Tract Tissues by Variant Subpopulations of Salmonella Enteritidis")]. Additionally, ovarian infection with Bacterium Pullorum in domestic fowl has been specifically noted, highlighting the impact of this pathogen on the reproductive organs [[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7e4cecab2a40f32592b90d2dbbeaee6056807d "Notes on Ovarian Infection with Bacterium Pullorum (Rettger) in the Domestic Fowl.")]. These findings underscore the significance of Pullorum disease in poultry, particularly in relation to the reproductive health of hens and the potential for vertical transmission of the disease.

Pullorum Disease, Ovarian Infection, and Hens: Mechanisms and Impacts
Pullorum disease, caused by Salmonella pullorum, is a significant bacterial infection in poultry, particularly affecting hens' reproductive systems. The disease not only reduces reproductive performance but also enables vertical transmission of the pathogen from hen to egg, posing ongoing challenges for poultry health and productivity.

Ovarian and Reproductive Tract Pathology
Salmonella pullorum can invade and persist in the ovaries and oviducts of hens, leading to abnormal follicle development, necrosis, and inflammation in reproductive tissues 1235813. Histological studies show deformed follicles, follicular dysplasia, and underdeveloped oviductal mucosa in infected hens 18. These changes are associated with decreased egg production, fertility, and hatchability 158.

Mechanisms of Persistence and Transmission
The bacterium can persist in splenic macrophages and reproductive tissues for extended periods, even in the absence of clinical disease 25813. At sexual maturity, bacterial numbers increase in the reproductive tract, facilitating transovarian transmission to eggs and chicks 258. Both horizontal (fecal-oral) and vertical (egg) transmission routes are documented 258.

Immune Modulation and Disease Progression
Salmonella pullorum modulates the host immune response, shifting from a Th 17-type to a Th 2-type response, which may promote persistent infection and facilitate reproductive tract colonization 45. At the onset of laying, a temporary suppression of cellular immunity in hens coincides with increased bacterial spread to the ovary and oviduct 5.

Summary
Salmonella pullorum infects the ovaries and oviducts of hens, causing reproductive tract damage, reduced egg production, and enabling vertical transmission to eggs and chicks. The pathogen’s persistence is aided by immune modulation and can be detected in reproductive tissues even in asymptomatic carriers. Understanding these mechanisms is crucial for effective control and prevention in poultry populations.
